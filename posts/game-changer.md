The other morning I overheard an enthusiastic woman telling a colleague about a product she’s been using at work. “It’s a total game changer” she said. She had accidentally become the best evangelist this product could have hoped for.

It got me wondering about my own product. Would somebody describe Chapter with the same passion and excitement? what would the product even look like to elicit a reaction like that? It turns out that this line of questioning is a great product north star.

Paul Graham famously says that to be a successful YC company you need to “build something people want”. It’s vague and hand-wavy, but that’s the point, there aren’t many universal rules when it comes to building successful products. It’s a great quip but it’s very tangible. I can’t picture it.

What is a game changing product? I’m sure you will have your own definition relating to your own product, that’s the beauty. For me,  it’s a one-way door, an epoch, there was a you before this product and a you after. You prefer the you after.

Some traits of products that have had this effect on my life:

- **Enablement**: The product has enabled me to do something that I wasn’t able to do before. Canva enables normal folks to design. Lovable enables non-engineers to build websites and software products. There’s a bunch that fit this category. Typically simplification is the lever here - as much as possible without sacrificing power.
- **Reframe**: A product reframes a problem or introduces a new primitive which completely goes against the status quo but turns out to be a critical unlock. Slack comes to mind. Figma, too. WisprFlow / Granola fit here for me.
- **10x**: This one’s a classic. Take an existing thing that is proven to be needed and useful and 10x it. Linear is a pure example of this.

Take a look at your own product. Is it game changing? if it’s not right now, why not?
