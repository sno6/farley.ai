The other morning I overheard an enthusiastic woman talking to a colleague about a product she’d been trialling. “It’s a total game changer” she said, unknowingly becoming the best evangelist this product could have ever hoped for.

It had me wondering about my own product. Would somebody call it game changing? What would a product even have to look like to elicit a reaction like that?

Paul Graham famously says that successful product companies ‘build something that people want’. It’s kind of vague and hand-wavy, but that’s the point, there aren’t many universal rules when it comes to building successful products. He’s right, of course. A necessary ingredient to hitting product market fit is building something that people find valuable, and finding pmf is a necessary ingredient to building a successful product company. My problem with the quote is that it’s not very actionable, from a product perspective.

But making a product game changing? That’s weirdly tangible, you can see and feel it. I’m sure if you closed your eyes and thought about your own product you could see what that game changing version looked like.

To me a game changing product is a one-way door. An epoch in time. There was a you before finding the product, and a you afterwards, and you’re never going back. There are obvious examples like the Spreadsheet, LLMs, and the iPhone, but the beauty of this framing is that it doesn’t necessarily care about how revolutionary your tech is, as long as it’s made an impact on how you do something you care about.

So what does a game changing product look like? Here are some of the traits that I have noticed in the products that I use that make this cut:

- **Enablement**: The product elevates one’s abilities to be able to do something they couldn’t do before. Canva enables design. Lovable enables software production. There’s a bunch that fit this category. Typically simplification is the lever here - as much as you can get away with without oversimplifying.
- **Novelty**: A product reframes a problem, or introduces a new primitive which leads to an obviously more valuable solution. Slack comes to mind. Figma, WisprFlow, Granola.
- **10x**: Take an existing product solution that is proven to be valuable and 10x it. Linear was a pretty pure example of this. It’s easier said than done, though, don’t fall into the trap of building a 10x solution to a problem nobody cares about.

What comes to your mind when you think about the game changing products in your life?
